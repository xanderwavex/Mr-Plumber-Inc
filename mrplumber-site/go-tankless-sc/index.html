What is a tankless water heater?

A traditional water heater is a big metal tank — usually 40 to 80 gallons — that keeps water hot 24 hours a day so it's ready when you need it. That means it's constantly using energy to reheat water you may not use for hours, and once the tank runs empty during a long shower or back-to-back loads of laundry, you wait 30–60 minutes for it to recover.

A tankless water heater (also called an "on-demand" water heater) skips the tank entirely. When you open a hot tap, cold water flows through the unit and past a powerful heat exchanger that warms it instantly. Close the tap and it shuts off. Because it heats water only as it flows, it can run essentially forever without running out, and it isn't paying to keep a tank hot in between.

Most units are about the size of a small suitcase and mount on a wall — in the garage, a closet, or outside. They run on natural gas, propane, or electricity, and a properly sized unit can supply multiple fixtures at once.

Your options

The tankless options we install

Which one fits depends on your home's fuel, your hot-water demand, and where the unit will live. We'll size it with you.

Gas (natural gas or propane)

The most common choice for whole-house use. Highest flow rates, works well for larger households. Needs proper gas supply and dedicated venting.

Electric

Simpler to install where gas isn't available. Great for a single bathroom, an addition, or point-of-use spots far from the main heater. May need an electrical panel upgrade.

Condensing

A high-efficiency gas unit that captures extra heat from its own exhaust, lowering operating cost. Uses PVC venting. Our usual recommendation for a main unit.

Whole-house

One correctly sized unit serving the entire home. The typical setup for a family replacing an aging tank.

Point-of-use

A small unit dedicated to one far-off fixture — a workshop sink, a guest bath, a wet bar — so you're not waiting for hot water to travel.

With a recirculation pump

Adds near-instant hot water at the tap and reduces water waste. A good add-on for larger homes with long pipe runs.

Why homeowners switch

The benefits of going tankless

Endless hot water

Showers, dishwasher, and laundry at the same time without anyone getting a cold surprise.

Lower energy bills

No standby loss from keeping a tank hot. Most homes see a meaningful drop in water-heating costs.

Lasts about twice as long

Roughly 20 years of service life versus 10–12 for a typical tank, and most parts are replaceable.

Reclaims your space

A wall-mounted unit frees up the footprint of a bulky tank in the garage or closet.

No tank to leak or flood

A failed storage tank can dump 50 gallons onto your floor. There's no tank here to rupture.

Cleaner hot water

Water isn't sitting for days in a tank collecting sediment and rust.

An honest comparison

Tank vs. tankless

Tankless isn't automatically right for every home. Here's the real trade-off.

Storage tank water heater vs. tankless water heater
  Storage tank Tankless
Hot water supply Limited to tank size; runs out, then needs 30–60 min to recover Con Continuous — heats on demand, doesn't run out Pro
Up-front cost Lower — cheaper unit and simpler install Pro Higher — unit plus possible gas, venting or electrical work Con
Energy use Standby loss 24/7 keeping the tank hot Con Only runs when water flows; typically lower bills Pro
Lifespan About 10–12 years Con About 20 years Pro
Space Large floor-standing tank Con Small wall-mounted unit Pro
Flood risk Tank can rupture and release 40–80 gallons Con No tank to fail Pro
Maintenance Occasional flush; whole unit replaced when it fails Pro Annual descaling recommended, especially in hard water Con
Simultaneous demand Fine until the tank empties Pro Must be sized correctly, or flow drops when many taps run Con
