# Mr. Plumber, Inc. — Website Style Guide

**Vibe in one line:** Four generations of trust, expressed through a modern, clean, "liquid glass" design language — not a flyer.

---

## Brand Positioning
- Heritage (1958, 4th generation family business) communicated through *tone and copy*, not through dated flyer/badge visuals
- Should feel premium, current, and trustworthy — closer to a modern service-business site than a typical local trade flyer
- Reference point: nearby competitor sites (e.g. wecorooter.com) are outdated — this site should clearly outclass them, not borrow from them

---

## Color Palette

White/light-dominant base, navy as the trust anchor (used sparingly, not in big blocks), warm muted red as a small accent for action items only.

| Role | Color | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Background (primary) | White / off-white (`#FFFFFF` / `#F8F9FB`) | Dominant color of the site — lots of breathing room |
| Anchor / Navy | Deep slate-navy (`#1B2A4A` range) | Nav bar, footer, headline text, key UI — muted/deeper than the old flyer navy, not neon-bright |
| Accent / Red | Muted brick-red or terracotta (`#C1503F` range) | CTA buttons, small icons, underlines/highlights ONLY — never large background fills |
| Neutral / Text | Charcoal gray (`#2B2E33` range) for body text; mid-gray for secondary text | Keeps text soft, not harsh black |
| Glass surfaces | White or navy at low opacity (e.g. `rgba(255,255,255,0.6)`, `rgba(27,42,74,0.4)`) with blur | Used for the liquid-glass panel effect (see Shape & Surface Language) |

**Rule of thumb:** if a section feels like it's "a lot of navy" or "a lot of red," pull it back — those colors are for anchoring and action, not filling space.

---

## Typography
- **Feel:** Clean and minimal — modern sans-serif throughout, not a trade-flyer bold-block look
- Headline weight can be a bit bolder/heavier for confidence, but stays clean (not condensed/industrial)
- Generous line-height and letter-spacing on headlines to support the airy, modern feel
- Suggested pairing direction: a modern grotesk/geometric sans (e.g. something in the Inter / Manrope / General Sans family) for both headlines and body — one type family, weight does the differentiation

---

## Imagery & Mascot

- **Mascot (cartoon plumber character):** Featured — appears large in the hero section, and reused in 1–2 other key spots (e.g. About/heritage section). Should be cleaned up / modernized in execution rather than looking like the old flyer version, even though the character itself stays.
- **Hero background:** Blurred, softened background image of an AI-generated fleet of branded Mr. Plumber trucks (client doesn't have a full fleet yet — this represents where the business is headed). Background sits behind a navy-tinted glass overlay with white headline text on top.
- **Service/section images:** Use placeholders for now (clean icon or blurred generic plumbing/work imagery) until real photos are available.
- **Icon style:** Simple, modern line or soft-solid icons for service categories — not the old circular badge-icon style from the flyer.
- **No literal badge/seal graphics** (e.g. "Serving the Midlands Since 1958" circular badge) — that heritage message comes through in copy and layout instead.

---

## Shape & Surface Language — "Liquid Glass"

- Panels, cards, and nav bar use a frosted-glass effect: semi-transparent background + backdrop blur + soft shadow + soft rounded corners
- **Corners:** Buttons = fully rounded/pill-shaped. Cards/panels = soft rounded corners (not sharp, not full pill). Nothing on the site should feel squared-off or industrial.
- **Depth:** Subtle layering — glass panels floating slightly above background imagery/color, soft drop shadows, no harsh borders
- **Buttons:** Primary CTA = solid accent-red pill button, white text. Secondary CTA = glass/outline pill button.

---

## Motion & Animation
- Smooth scroll-triggered animations as the user scrolls down the page (sections/cards fade or slide gently into view)
- Motion should feel fluid and "flowing," not snappy/abrupt — supports the liquid-glass feel
- Hero elements (headline, mascot, CTA) can have a soft entrance animation on page load
- Avoid excessive or distracting motion — animation should support scannability, not compete with it

---

## Layout & Density
- **Density:** Non-cluttered, comfortable whitespace — but not maximalist/empty either. Enough breathing room to feel premium while still surfacing services and info without excessive scrolling per section.
- **Mobile-first — this is a priority.** Every section must be designed to work cleanly on a phone: single-column stacking, thumb-friendly button sizes, readable text without zooming, fast-loading blurred images.
- Content should be structured for easy scrolling — clear section breaks, not one dense wall of information.
